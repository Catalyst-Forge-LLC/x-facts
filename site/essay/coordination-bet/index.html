The coordination bet

If you want to help xFacts get adopted, maintained, integrated, or talked about honestly, this page is for you.

Not linked from the hub home page · Catalyst Forge · August 2026

Looking for how to use labels as a reader or publisher? Start with Why labels instead.

xFacts is a family of small, validatable nutrition labels for the AI stack: app, model, tool, agent, skill. Specs are CC0. The bet is simple: trust at the moment of adoption is underserved, and the reader of that trust signal is increasingly a machine.

That bet only pays off if people like you close the loop between publishing, discovering, and acting on labels. This note is a map of useful help, not a pitch deck for bystanders.

The gap is real. Adoption is not automatic. Help means making labels emitted, findable, and load-bearing, without turning them into theater.

Who this is for

  • Adopters — you can put a label next to something people install.
  • Integrators — you build hosts, harnesses, CI, templates, or agent bootstraps.
  • Maintainers — you care about specs, validators, generators, directories.
  • Broadcasters — you write, teach, demo, or brief teams on the AI stack.
  • Evangelists — you want the vocabulary to spread even if the domains do not stay “ours.”

Where help has leverage

Ordered by impact, not by how easy the pull request is:

  1. Make a label load-bearing. Wire ToolFacts (or AgentFacts) into a real approval or attach flow. Auto-approve read-only idempotent tools; gate writes; always prompt on destructive. One host that acts on facts beats a hundred unread READMEs.
  2. Emit from reality. Generators that fill labels from MCP tools/list, skill packages, model cards, or app manifests. Hand-authored virtue does not scale.
  3. Seed useful catalogs. Empty directories kill network effects. ModelFacts-style indexes with honest undisclosed beat sparse marketing pages.
  4. Point agents at the files. Put hub and label llms.txt links in AGENTS.md templates, ForgeKit-style bootstraps, and host docs. Agents only discover what you teach them to fetch.
  5. Dogfood a full panel. Ship App + Model + Tool + Agent (+ Skill) on something real. End demos on a portable viewer card. Composition is the suite’s picture.
  6. Normalize CI. Schema validate and staleness checks in greenfield templates so unlabeled starts to feel incomplete.
  7. Talk clearly. Explain the decision question, not “AI ethics theater.” Send readers to Why labels. Keep xFacts lowercase-x framing (variable, not the social network).

What “success” can look like

Official labels from labs and hosts matter more than Catalyst Forge owning the conversation. If someone adopts the vocabulary and schemas under another roof, that can still be a win. Specs are CC0 on purpose: low friction to copy, fork, or absorb.

Sponsored placement in directories is not a win. Neither is “certified” language before anyone can measure claims. Prefer harsher enums when unsure. A wrong side_effects: none is worse than no label.

What not to do

  • Do not invent new xFacts labels that fail the admission rule (objective, derivable, no good incumbent).
  • Do not treat shareable /v#… cards as proof. They are for passing a view around; git + schema stay canonical.
  • Do not label every host permission tweak as a new agent. Label shipped defaults.
  • Do not oversell self-reported autonomy or safety as calibrated scores.

Where to plug in

  • Hub: xfacts.dev · family pointer /llms.txt
  • Consumer essay: Why labels
  • Maintainer analysis: suite value assessment
  • Sequencing: ROADMAPS.md and each label’s specs/IMPROVEMENT-ROADMAP.md
  • Label sites: AppFacts, ModelFacts, ToolFacts, AgentFacts, SkillFacts (see hub)

If you only do one thing: pick a surface you already control (a template, a host, a demo, a skill marketplace, a CI starter) and make an xFacts label either emitted, required, or consulted there. That is how a correctly timed idea becomes infrastructure.
